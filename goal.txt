Goal

Does GPT-2, and a second open model, treat a denial of a past habit as more
surprising after stop than after a control verb that does not presuppose that
habit? The cost should remain when stop is negated, because a presupposition
survives negation.

The critical word is never, in sentences such as:

  Amy stopped smoking. Amy had never smoked before.
  Amy did not stop smoking. Amy had never smoked before.
  Amy mentioned smoking. Amy had never smoked before.
  Amy did not mention smoking. Amy had never smoked before.

Mentioned is the control. The prediction is a positive gap: surprisal of never
after stop, minus surprisal of never after mentioned. The gap that has to hold
is the negated one, did not stop versus did not mention.

Why the critical word is not the verb

A first test put the contradictory context in front of the verb:

  Amy used to smoke. Amy stopped smoking.
  Amy has never smoked. Amy stopped smoking.

and compared stopped with mentioned. Has never made both verbs more surprising.
The extra rise for stopped was 0.05 bits in GPT-2 and 0.21 bits in Pythia-160M.
Both confidence intervals included zero. A negative context alone is not
evidence of the presupposition, so the scored word was moved to the later denial.

Result that matches this goal

On 24 habits, surprisal of never after did not stop exceeds surprisal after
did not mention.

  GPT-2:       0.47 bits, 24 of 24 habits, interval excludes zero.
  Pythia-160M: 0.56 bits, 23 of 24 habits, interval excludes zero.

Both models show the presupposition cost for negated stop.

Limits, not the main claim

The affirmative sentence stopped shows the gap in GPT-2 (0.50 bits, 22 of 24)
but not in Pythia-160M. The same negation test with started does not show the
predicted gap in either model. The claim is about negated stop, not about
aspectual verbs in general. Henry (chess) and Kara (poetry) are the two GPT-2
habits in which affirmative stopped did not exceed mentioned.
